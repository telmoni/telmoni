Telmoni has two tiers: **Organizations** and **Projects**. There are no teams.

An **Organization** is the top level. It holds projects, organization-level
members, organization settings, and a rolled-up audit log.

A **Project** lives inside an organization. It holds API keys, notification
connectors (Slack, Discord, webhooks), in-app notifications, and project-specific
audit records.

A solo developer and an enterprise work the same way: an organization with one
member and a project, or one with many members across multiple projects.

## Your organizations and projects

When you first sign in, Telmoni provisions **an Organization**, with you as
its **Owner** (see [Signing in](/docs/getting-started/sign-in) for the switch an
operator can turn this off with). It arrives named after the first word of
your name ("Ada's organization"), or "My organization" when your account has
no name; [Signing in](/docs/getting-started/sign-in#what-your-first-sign-in-creates)
says where the name comes from. The name is what everyone in it sees, and its
URL follows from it (see [Name and URL](#name-and-url)). Nothing is asked
first: you can change both in **Settings** whenever you like.

The organization starts with no projects. The console opens on its
**Overview** (`/{organization}`), which lists the organization's projects and
is where you [create the first one](#creating-a-project); API keys and
connectors belong to a project, so nothing else is there to do until it
exists.

A sign-in opens the Overview of your
[default organization](/docs/account/settings#default-organization), or returns
you to the page that sent you to sign in. Within a session, opening the
console (`/console`) returns to the Overview of the organization you were last
in.

You can belong to multiple organizations at once: ones you own, including any
you [create](#creating-an-organization), and ones you have been invited to.
Your role in one organization does not dictate your role in another.

### Roles at a glance

Both organizations and projects use three roles: **Owner**, **Admin**, and
**Member**. See [Roles and permissions](/docs/workspace/roles) for the full
permissions matrix.

- **Organization Owner**: Full access across all projects, project creation and
  deletion, member management, organization settings, and organization
  transfer/deletion.
- **Organization Admin**: Can create projects, manage members and organization
  settings, and read the organization's audit log. Operates as an admin across
  all projects. Cannot delete projects, delete the organization, or transfer
  ownership.
- **Organization Member**: Access is restricted to the specific projects they are
  seated in. No organization-wide management powers.

## Moving between them

The console's address names the organization, and the project under it, by a
**slug**: a short URL-safe handle, such as `acme`. An organization's is set
when it is created, as the URL you give it or one derived from its name, and
changed only in its Settings; a project's follows its name (see [Name and
URL](#name-and-url)). On these pages,
`{organization}` and `{project}` stand for those two slugs.

- **An organization's overview** is `/{organization}`, which lists its
  projects: every one to its Owner and Admins, and to a Member the ones they
  hold a seat on.
- **Organization pages** sit beside its projects, at words no project goes by:
  `/{organization}/projects`, `/{organization}/members`,
  `/{organization}/audit-log`, and `/{organization}/settings`, with
  `/{organization}/billing` on the hosted service.
- **Project pages** sit under the organization: `/{organization}/{project}` is
  the project's overview, with `/api-keys`, `/connectors`, `/members`,
  `/audit-log`, and `/settings` under it.
- **Account pages** live under `/account`, including `/account/settings`,
  `/account/notifications`, and `/account/privacy`.
- **Switch between projects and organizations** using the switcher in the
  console header. It lists your organizations and the projects within each,
  and ends with **New organization**, below **Create project** when you can
  create a project somewhere.

### Name and URL

An organization has a **name**, which everyone in it sees, and a **URL**, the
slug its addresses begin with. They are two settings.

- A slug is lowercase ASCII letters and digits, in words joined by hyphens, at
  most 48 characters long.
- **A new organization starts with a URL.** You can give one when you [create
  the organization](#creating-an-organization); otherwise it is derived from
  the name it is created with: accents fold and apostrophes
  drop, so "Zoë's organization" becomes `zoes-organization`, and "My
  organization" `my-organization`. A URL already in use gets a number, up to `-20`
  (`zoes-organization-2`); past that it ends in six random characters
  (`zoes-organization-k3x9qz`). When the first word of your name has no Latin
  letter or digit ("李明's organization"), the organization gets a placeholder
  (`org-` and ten random characters) until you choose a URL.
- **Renaming an organization moves nothing.** Its URL stays as it is, and so
  does every link to its pages.
- **Changing the URL** is done on the organization's **Settings** page, under
  **URL**: any slug not held by another organization and not one of the
  console's own words. Every page of the organization and of its projects moves
  to the new address, and the old address answers **Not found**. Pages open in
  the console follow the change; links made before it do not.
- **A project's URL follows its name.** Within its organization a project's slug
  is unique; a name already taken as a slug gets a number. Some words are kept
  for the organization's own pages (`projects`, `members`, `settings`, and
  others), and a name that is one gets the numbered form: `Settings` becomes
  `settings-2`. A name with no Latin letters or digits gets a placeholder
  (`project-` and ten random characters). Renaming a project moves its pages,
  and the old address answers **Not found**; a new name that gives no slug
  leaves the URL as it was.
- An organization's URL is unique across all organizations, ones awaiting
  deletion included; once a deleted organization is purged, another may take
  its URL.
- **IDs never move.** An organization's ID (`org_...`) and a project's ID
  (`project_...`) are unchanged by any rename or URL change, and a console path
  spelled with an ID in place of a slug redirects to the current slugs, keeping
  the rest of the path. Use the ID in anything you script. An organization's ID
  is shown on its **Settings** page, which opens for its Owner and Admins
  (`telmoni org list`, after a browser `telmoni login`, prints it for anyone in
  it); a project's on its
  **Settings** page and on the organization's **Projects** page.

### Console navigation and keyboard shortcuts

The web console provides built-in keyboard navigation (`⌘` is `Ctrl` on
Windows and Linux):

- **Quick search (`⌘K` or `/`)**: Opens the search palette: recently visited
  pages, projects, documentation, and, on a project's pages, that project's API
  keys and members.
- **Ask the agent (`⌘J`)**: Opens the **Telmoni Agent** panel ([the console
  agent](/docs/self-host/agent)) from any project view. Ask questions about project
  activity, members, connectors, or audit logs. Responses stream with inline
  citations and links to referenced sources. Past conversations are saved per
  person and per project: the history lists the ones you asked from the project
  you are on, and each can be reopened or deleted.
- **Account menu (`⌘⇧K`)**: Opens the user menu: your account, the home
  page, the documentation, the keyboard shortcuts sheet, theme and corners,
  the platform's GitHub repository, and sign out.
- **Primary action (`c`)**: Triggers the primary action of the current page:
  **New project**, **Mint key**, or **Invite member**.
- **Navigation sequences (`g` followed by a key)**, relative to the project
  you are in:
  - `g` `o`: Overview
  - `g` `k`: API keys
  - `g` `c`: Connectors
  - `g` `m`: Members
  - `g` `l`: Audit log
  - `g` `p`: Settings
  - `g` `O`: The organization's overview, from anywhere in the console

  On an organization's own pages, `g` `o`, `g` `m`, `g` `l`, and `g` `p` open
  the organization's Overview, Members, Audit log, and Settings, and `g` `k`
  and `g` `c` do nothing.
- **Keyboard shortcuts cheat sheet (`?`)**: Opens the full shortcuts sheet, with
  a switch to enable or disable single-key shortcuts.

## Projects

### Creating a project

An organization Owner or Admin can create a new project from the organization's
**Overview** (`/{organization}`), its **Projects** page
(`/{organization}/projects`), or the switcher:

1. Select **New project** on the Overview or the Projects page, or **Create
   project** in the switcher. When you own or administer more than one
   organization, the dialog asks which one the project lands in, starting from
   the Overview's own organization when you opened it there.
2. Provide a project name: up to 100 characters, and not one another project in
   the organization already has (compared without regard to case).
3. The project is created immediately. Its API keys and connectors are its own.

### Renaming a project

An Admin or Owner can rename a project from the project's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/{project}/settings`). The same limits apply as at creation,
and the rename moves the project's URL (see [Name and URL](#name-and-url)).

### Deleting a project

Only the **Owner** can delete a project. Deletion is initiated from the
**Danger zone** on the project's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/{project}/settings`):

- The dialog asks you to type the project's name before it deletes anything.
- It takes effect at once, with no undo.
- Deleting a project deletes its API keys, member seats, and pending
  invitations with it, and its connectors, their delivery history, and its
  activity feed are purged right after (whatever fails then goes when the
  organization itself is purged). The console agent removes what it holds
  about the project.
- Historical audit events for that project remain preserved in the organization's
  append-only audit chain.

### Transferring a project

An organization Owner can hand a project to one of the project's Admins:

1. The Owner selects **Transfer project** on the Admin's row on the project's
   **Members** page (`/{organization}/{project}/members`) and confirms with
   **Send offer**. Only a project Admin can be offered a project.
2. The Admin is emailed, and the offer appears in their console bell and under
   **Project offers** on their account's **Notifications** page
   (`/account/notifications`). It stands for **7 days**, and a project has one
   live offer at a time: a new offer withdraws the earlier one, and whoever
   held it is told. The Owner can withdraw it with **Withdraw offer** on the
   same row; removing the Admin from the project, or their leaving it, ends the
   offer too. The Owner is emailed a decline, and the Admin a withdrawal.
3. The Admin accepts the project into an organization they own. The page asks
   which one when they own several, and offers only **Decline** when they own
   none.
4. On acceptance:
   - The project row moves into the new organization. It keeps its name unless
     that organization already has a project by it, in which case it arrives
     with the next free number on the end (`Payments 2`). Its URL changes with
     the move (see [Name and URL](#name-and-url)).
   - Its API keys follow into the new organization. The offer on the
     Notifications page advises the new owner to rotate any key they did not
     mint.
   - Project seats and pending invitations stay on the project, and everyone
     seated is enrolled on the new organization's roster as a **Member**, as a
     project invitation would enrol them. The accepting Admin's own seat is
     folded into their ownership.
   - The former owner stays on the project as an **Admin**, joins the new
     organization as a **Member**, and is emailed that the project moved.
   - **Connectors do not follow**: a project's Slack and Discord grants and
     webhook secrets are the old organization's, so they are removed before
     the move commits, with their delivery history and the project's activity
     feed, and a removal that fails moves nothing. The console agent removes
     what it holds about the project in the old organization.
   - Both organizations' audit logs record the move; the receiving one also
     notes the name the project was offered under when a clash renamed it. The
     project's earlier events stay in the old organization's log, so its
     **Audit log** page starts at the move.

## Organizations

### Creating an organization

Anyone signed in to the console can create another organization, and becomes
its **Owner**:

1. Open the switcher in the console header and select **New organization**.
2. Give it a **Name**: up to 80 characters, the name everyone in it sees.
3. Give it a **URL**, or leave the field blank to have one derived from the
   name, as [Name and URL](#name-and-url) describes: "Acme Robotics" becomes
   `acme-robotics`, with a number on the end when another organization holds
   that, and a name with no Latin letter or digit gets a placeholder (`org-`
   and ten random characters) until you choose a URL. A URL you give must be
   a slug no other organization holds and not one of the console's own words.
4. Select **Create organization**. The console opens the new organization's
   **Overview**, where you [create its first project](#creating-a-project).

There is no limit on how many organizations you create or own. Creating one
leaves your [default organization](/docs/account/settings#default-organization),
the one a sign-in opens, as it was. An operator who has [closed new
organizations](/docs/self-host/production#closing-new-organizations) closes this
too: the switcher offers no **New organization**.

### Renaming an organization

An Owner or Admin can rename an organization from its **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/settings`). The name can be up to 80 characters. A rename
moves nothing: the organization's URL is its own setting on the same page (see
[Name and URL](#name-and-url)).

### Transferring organization ownership

An organization has exactly one Owner. The Owner can transfer ownership to any
organization Admin:

1. The Owner selects **Transfer ownership** on the Admin's row on the
   organization's **Members** page (`/{organization}/members`) and confirms
   with **Send offer**.
2. The Admin is emailed, and the offer appears in their console bell and on
   their account's **Notifications** page (`/account/notifications`). It stands
   for **7 days**, and an organization has one live offer at a time: a new
   offer withdraws the earlier one, and whoever held it is told. The Owner may
   withdraw the offer at any time before it is accepted; removing the Admin
   from the organization, or their leaving it, ends the offer too.
3. The Admin accepts (confirming with **Become the owner**) or declines on the
   Notifications page. The Owner is emailed either answer, and the Admin is
   emailed a withdrawal.
4. Upon acceptance:
   - The accepting Admin becomes the new **Owner**. Any project seats they held
     are removed, since the Owner's standing on every project comes from the
     organization.
   - The former owner becomes an **Admin** in the organization, and may then
     leave it.
   - All projects, API keys, connectors, and audit histories stay intact. The
     handover, and each removed seat, is written to the audit log.

### Deleting an organization

Only the **Owner** can delete an organization. Deletion is performed from the
**Danger zone** in the organization's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/settings`):

1. Request a 6-digit confirmation code, which is emailed to the Owner's address
   and remains valid for 15 minutes. Five wrong guesses burn it; **Resend
   code** issues another. The console sends at most three codes an hour and
   takes ten attempts an hour. On a self-hosted deployment, the code goes out
   only when the operator has configured mail (`SMTP_URL`); without it, the
   organization cannot be deleted from the console.
2. Enter the confirmation code.
3. **Access ends immediately**: the organization is closed, all API keys stop
   authenticating, and member access ceases. On the hosted service, its
   billing is closed at once; a part that cannot finish is retried every ten
   minutes, and the confirmation screen says so when that happens. Your own
   account is untouched, and if this was your last organization, your next
   console page provisions a new one while new organizations are allowed, as
   your [first
   sign-in](/docs/getting-started/sign-in#what-your-first-sign-in-creates) did.
4. **Erasure follows, usually within the hour**: the automated deletion sweep
   permanently purges the organization, its projects, API keys, connectors,
   notices and console agent data. There is no restore window and no undo.

An organization an operator closed from the server (`telmoni terminate`, on a
self-hosted instance) goes the same way.

## Leaving an organization or project

- **Leaving an organization**: Anyone but the Owner can leave from the
  organization's **Overview** page (`/{organization}`), with **Leave
  organization**. Leaving removes you from the organization and from every
  project in it.
- **Leaving a project**: The organization's **Projects** page
  (`/{organization}/projects`) offers **Leave** on each project you hold a
  seat on. That page opens only for the organization's Owner and Admins, and
  leaving gives up the seat alone: they keep their access to every project
  through their organization role. An organization Member cannot open the
  page: with a project's Admin seat, they can remove themselves on the
  project's **Members** page, and with a Member seat they leave a project only
  by leaving the organization.
- The **Owner cannot leave** an active organization. To leave, the Owner must
  either transfer ownership to an Admin or delete the organization.
