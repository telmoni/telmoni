Telmoni has two tiers: **Organizations** and **Projects**. There are no teams.

An **Organization** is the top level. It holds projects, organization-level
members, organization settings, and a rolled-up audit log.

A **Project** lives inside an organization. It holds API keys, notification
connectors (Slack, webhooks), in-app notifications, project-specific
audit records, and its [content mode](#a-projects-content-mode).

A solo developer and an enterprise work the same way: an organization with one
member and a project, or one with many members across multiple projects.

## Your organizations and projects

When you first sign in, Telmoni provisions **an Organization**, with you as
its **Owner** (see [Signing in](/docs/getting-started/sign-in) for the switch an
operator can turn this off with). It arrives named the part of your email
address before the `@` ("ada" for `ada@acme.com`);
[Signing in](/docs/getting-started/sign-in#what-your-first-sign-in-creates)
says where the name comes from. The name is what everyone in it sees, and its
URL follows from it (see [Name and URL](#name-and-url)). Nothing is asked
first: you can change both in **Settings** whenever you like.

The organization starts with no projects. The console opens on its
**Projects** page (`/{organization}/projects`);
[create the first project](#creating-a-project) with **New project** there or
from the switcher. API keys and connectors belong to a project, so there is little
else to do until one exists.

A sign-in opens the Projects page of your
[default organization](/docs/settings/account#default-organization), or returns
you to the page that sent you to sign in. Within a session, opening the
console (`/console`) returns to the Projects page of the organization you were
last in.

You can belong to multiple organizations at once: ones you own, including any
you [create](#creating-an-organization), and ones you have been invited to.
Your role in one organization does not dictate your role in another.

### Roles at a glance

Both organizations and projects use three roles: **Owner**, **Admin**, and
**Member**. See [Roles and permissions](/docs/workspace/roles) for the full
permissions matrix.

- **Organization Owner**: Full access across all projects, project creation and
  deletion, member management, organization settings, and organization
  transfer/deletion.
- **Organization Admin**: Can create projects, manage members and organization
  settings, and read the organization's audit log. Operates as an admin across
  all projects. Cannot delete projects, delete the organization, or transfer
  ownership.
- **Organization Member**: Access is restricted to the specific projects they are
  seated in. No organization-wide management powers.

## Moving between them

The console's address names the organization, and the project under it, by a
**slug**: a short URL-safe handle, such as `acme`. An organization's is set
when it is created, as the URL you give it or one derived from its name, and
changed only in its Settings; a project's follows its name (see [Name and
URL](#name-and-url)). On these pages,
`{organization}` and `{project}` stand for those two slugs.

- **An organization opens on its projects**: `/{organization}` opens
  `/{organization}/projects`, the projects you can open, by name, with **New
  project** for its Owner and Admins and **Leave organization** at the foot
  for everyone but the Owner. Its projects are in the switcher too.
- **Organization pages** sit beside its projects, at words no project goes by:
  `/{organization}/projects`, `/{organization}/members`,
  `/{organization}/audit-log`, `/{organization}/notifications`,
  `/{organization}/limits` ([its limits](#dividing-its-limits-among-its-projects)),
  `/{organization}/data-sheet` (the [data sheet](/docs/workspace/data-sheet))
  and `/{organization}/settings`,
  with `/{organization}/billing` on the hosted service.
- **Project pages** sit under the organization: `/{organization}/{project}` is
  the project's [agents](#a-projects-agents), each with its last trace, what it
  cost and the alerts that watch it, with
  [`/traces`](/docs/monitoring/traces), **Traces** — every trace
  of the project's agents, grouped by session or user where asked, and a page
  for each — [`/alerts`](/docs/monitoring/alerts) — its alerts and
  their incidents — `/objectives` — [`/dashboards`](/docs/monitoring/dashboards),
  **Spend** — its spend, tokens, traces, latency and time to first token over a
  window, and its budgets — and `/api-keys`, `/connectors`,
  `/members`, `/audit-log`, and `/settings` under it — its content mode, how long its
  data is kept, and an end user's data given or erased.
- **Your settings** live under `/settings`: `/settings/general`,
  `/settings/account`, `/settings/notifications`, `/settings/accessibility`,
  and `/settings/privacy`.
- **Switch between projects and organizations** using the switcher in the
  console header: two menus side by side. The organization's name opens your
  organizations, ending with **New organization**. Beside it, the project's
  name — or **Select a project** on the organization's own pages — opens that
  organization's projects, ending with **New project** for its Owner and
  Admins.

### Name and URL

An organization has a **name**, which everyone in it sees, and a **URL**, the
slug its addresses begin with. They are two settings.

- A slug is lowercase ASCII letters and digits, in words joined by hyphens, at
  most 48 characters long.
- **A new organization starts with a URL.** You can give one when you [create
  the organization](#creating-an-organization); otherwise it is derived from
  the name it is created with: accents fold and apostrophes drop, so "Zoë's
  organization" becomes `zoes-organization`. When another organization
  already holds that, or it is a word the console uses, six random characters
  go on the end (`zoes-organization-k3x9qz`), never a number, so a URL never
  says how many others hold the name. When the part of your address before the `@` has no Latin letter or
  digit ("李明"), the organization gets a placeholder
  (`org-` and ten random characters) until you choose a URL. So does a name
  whose URL would hold profanity or a slur, which no URL may: the name itself
  is kept as given.
- **Renaming an organization moves nothing.** Its URL stays as it is, and so
  does every link to its pages.
- **Changing the URL** is done on the organization's **Settings** page, under
  **URL**: any slug not held by another organization, not one of the console's
  own words, and holding no profanity or slur. Every page of the organization and of its projects moves
  to the new address, and the old address answers **Not found**. Pages open in
  the console follow the change; links made before it do not.
- **A project's URL follows its name.** Within its organization a project's slug
  is unique; a name already taken as a slug gets a number. Some words are kept
  for the organization's own pages (`projects`, `members`, `settings`, and
  others), and a name that is one gets the numbered form: `Settings` becomes
  `settings-2`. A name with no Latin letters or digits, or one whose URL would
  hold profanity or a slur, gets a placeholder (`project-` and ten random
  characters). Renaming a project moves its pages,
  and the old address answers **Not found**; a new name that gives no slug
  leaves the URL as it was.
- An organization's URL is unique across all organizations, ones awaiting
  deletion included; once a deleted organization is purged, another may take
  its URL.
- **IDs never move.** An organization's ID (`org_...`) and a project's ID
  (`project_...`) are unchanged by any rename or URL change, and a console path
  spelled with an ID in place of a slug redirects to the current slugs, keeping
  the rest of the path. Use the ID in anything you script. An organization's ID
  is shown on its **Settings** page, which opens for its Owner and Admins
  (`telmoni org list`, after a browser `telmoni login`, prints it for anyone in
  it); a project's on its **Settings** page.

### Console navigation and keyboard shortcuts

The web console provides built-in keyboard navigation (`⌘` is `Ctrl` on
Windows and Linux):

- **Quick search (`⌘K` or `/`)**: Opens the search palette: recently visited
  pages, projects, documentation, and, on a project's pages, that project's API
  keys and members.
- **Ask the agent (`⌘J`)**: Opens the **Telmoni Agent** window ([the console
  agent](/docs/self-host/agent)) over the page you are on, where you last left
  it. Drag it by its title bar, size it from any edge or corner, or fill the
  screen from its header or with a double-click on its title bar. Ask questions
  about a project's activity, members, connectors, or audit log; a new
  conversation offers a few to start with. Responses stream with inline
  citations and links to referenced sources, and **Stop** ends one early,
  without saving it. Past conversations are saved per person and per project:
  the history lists the ones you asked from the project you are on, and each
  can be reopened or deleted. A conversation stays open on the organization's
  own pages; another project's pages start a new one.
- **Account menu (`⌘⇧K`)**: Opens the user menu: your settings, the home
  page, the documentation, the keyboard shortcuts sheet, the platform's GitHub
  repository, and sign out. The theme and corners are set on the
  [General](/docs/settings/general) settings page.
- **Primary action (`c`)**: Triggers the primary action of the current page:
  **New project**, **Mint key**, **Invite member** (on a project's **Members**
  page, **Add member**), **New alert** on a project's **Alerts** page, **New objective** on its
  **Objectives** page, or
  **Export** on the organization's audit log; on the hosted service's
  **Billing** page, **Plans** or **Manage billing**.
- **Next and previous (`j`, `k`), open (`o`)**: With a trace open over the
  [Traces](/docs/monitoring/traces#a-trace) list, open the next row's trace or
  the previous one's. After `g`, `k` is still API keys and `o` a project's Agents.
- **Navigation sequences (`g` followed by a key)**: every row of the sidebar
  you are in has one, and the sheet (`?`) lists the rows of that sidebar. On a
  project's pages:
  - `g` `o`: Agents
  - `g` `t`: Traces, `g` `a`: Alerts, `g` `v`: Objectives, `g` `s`: Spend
  - `g` `k`: API keys, `g` `c`: Connectors, `g` `m`: Members, `g` `l`: Audit log, `g` `n`: Notifications
  - `g` `,`: Settings, as `⌘,` opens preferences on a Mac

  On an organization's pages: `g` `p` Projects, `g` `m` Members, `g` `l`
  Audit log, `g` `n` Notifications, `g` `i` Limits, `g` `,` Settings, and on
  the hosted service `g` `b` Billing. On your account's pages: `g` `,`
  General, `g` `u` Account, `g` `n` Notifications, `g` `a` Accessibility,
  `g` `p` Privacy. A row that is not on the sidebar you are in has no sequence
  there, so `g` `k` on an organization's pages does nothing.
  A console built on this one can add rows of its own, each with a key of its
  own. Two sequences work from anywhere in the console:
  - `g` `n`: Notifications — a project's on its pages, an organization's on
    its own, and yours on your account's
  - `g` `O` (shift): The organization's projects
- **Keyboard shortcuts cheat sheet (`?`, `Shift` `/` on a US keyboard)**: Opens the full shortcuts sheet.
  Letter-key shortcuts can be turned off on the
  [Accessibility](/docs/settings/accessibility) page; the sheet then lists the
  `⌘` shortcuts alone.

A panel over the whole page, such as a new alert or a trace at full size,
holds off `c` and the sequences until it shuts, and the sheet and the hints
below leave them out meanwhile; its own `j`, `k` and `o`, Search and the sheet
still answer. A letter pressed on a form in a side panel is the form's own.

Hover over a sidebar row, or over search, the bell, the agent or your account
in the header, to see its shortcut. With letter-key shortcuts off, only the
`⌘` ones are shown.

The foot of the sidebar shows the version of Telmoni the console runs.

## Projects

### A project's agents

A project opens on its **Agents**, `/{organization}/{project}`, the first row
of its sidebar, its tab titled by the project's name: every agent that ran
there in the last 14 days, the one whose last trace started latest first.
Anyone on the project reads it:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Agent | Its name, a link to its traces on **Traces**; under it, its newest [change](/docs/monitoring/traces#changes) of each kind in the 14 days — a release, a model, a prompt's version or a first tool call — each naming its environment and opening its trace beside the agent's traces |
| Last trace | How the trace that started last ended, or **Running**, and when it started, a link that opens it beside the agent's traces on **Traces** |
| Traces | Its traces over the 14 days, and how many ended in error |
| Last trace's cost | What that trace cost, priced as **Traces** prices it, and what the agent's ended traces usually cost, their median |
| Watched by | The [alerts](/docs/monitoring/alerts) that read its traces, those held to it and those over every agent, paused ones aside, and the open incidents of those held to it |

Each row ends in **Versions**, which opens the agent's
[versions side by side](#an-agents-versions-side-by-side). The open incidents
of alerts over every agent, which no one agent's row
counts, are told once above the table. The page draws itself again as the
project's spans land, while it is in view: at once for the first, and once
more for whatever arrives in the next 30 seconds. A project where no agent
has run in the 14 days says how to send its first trace. A project's notices are on its
**Notifications** page, its budgets on **Spend**, and its name, ID and
[content mode](#a-projects-content-mode) on its **Settings**.

### An agent's versions, side by side

**Versions** on an agent's row opens a panel beside the page that sets the
agent's versions side by side, to answer whether a new one made its traces fail
more, run longer, cost more or score lower. Three tabs split its traces:
**Releases**, **Models**, and **Prompts**, one prompt's versions at a time,
the prompt most of its traces name first and the others in a picker. The panel
reads the same 14 days, or as many as your plan and the project's
[retention](/docs/workspace/data-retention) keep of its spans where that is
fewer, and says how many.

A row is one version, and the traces that carried it:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Release, Model or Version | The version, and when the first of its traces in those days started. That is when it was first seen in the window, not when it was deployed |
| Traces | The traces that carried it, those still running among them |
| Failed | The share of them that ended in error |
| Median, p95 | The 50th and 95th percentiles of how long its ended traces took, as **Traces** times a trace |
| Median cost | The median of what its ended traces cost, priced as **Traces** prices them |
| One for each score | The mean of that [score](/docs/monitoring/scores) on its traces, a yes or no counted as 1 or 0: up to five scores, those given most often, and none of a category |

The rows stand newest first seen first. Under each number is its difference
from the row below, the version first seen before it: a failed share's in
percentage points, a duration's and a cost's in their own units, a score's on
the score's own scale. A rise in failures, duration or cost is drawn in the
alert's colour and a fall in green; a score's is left plain, since Telmoni
does not know whether higher is better. Nothing is inferred from a
difference, and none is drawn where either row has no value, as a version
with no ended trace has no duration or cost, nor a difference in its failed
share. The oldest row has none. A version already running before those days
is first seen at their start, so versions that old stand in the order their
first traces in the window came, and the difference between two of them says
nothing of which came first.

- **One environment at a time.** The rows are of one environment, the one most
  of the agent's traces were sent from unless you pick another; the picker
  shows where there are two or more, with **(no environment)** for spans that
  name none. A trace belongs to the versions its spans in that environment
  carry, and is measured whole: its failure, its duration, its cost and its
  scores are the trace's.
- **A trace can count twice.** A trace that called two models counts under
  each, as one whose spans carry two releases or two versions of a prompt does.
- **(none).** The traces whose spans in the environment carried no release, no
  model or, of the prompt's traces, no version are the last row, measured
  against no other.
- **Fifty rows.** The panel names the fifty versions first seen latest, and
  says so where the days held more.

### Creating a project

An organization Owner or Admin can create a new project from the organization's
**Projects** page (`/{organization}/projects`) or the switcher's project menu:

1. Select **New project** on the Projects page, or **New project** in the
   switcher's project menu. A panel opens over the page, as a trace opens
   over its list at full size: it names the organization the project lands
   in, the one you are in, and asks for a name and its
   [content mode](#a-projects-content-mode). Escape, **Cancel** or its close
   button shuts it, and so does moving to another page.
2. Provide a project name: up to 100 characters, and not one another project in
   the organization already has (compared without regard to case).
3. Leave the content mode **Off**, as it starts, or, as the organization's
   Owner, choose **On**.
4. The project is created immediately. Its API keys and connectors are its own.
   With **On** chosen, a code is mailed to the Owner, and the project keeps
   content once it is typed in the panel. If the message does not arrive,
   choose **Resend code**; **Keep it off** opens the project with content off
   instead.

A deployment may hold each organization to a number of projects, whatever its
plan: past it, creating a project, accepting one in a transfer or moving one
in is refused, the organization keeps every project it already holds, and the
console names who to write to for more. On the hosted service the number is 50
([Limits](https://telmoni.com/docs/workspace/billing#limits)); a self-hosted
deployment sets none.

### Renaming a project

An Admin or Owner can rename a project from the project's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/{project}/settings`). The same limits apply as at creation,
and the rename moves the project's URL (see [Name and URL](#name-and-url)).

### A project's content mode

A project's **content mode** says what it keeps of the content its spans
carry: the prompts and completions of an agent's model calls, the inputs and
outputs of its tool calls, error messages, a person's name or email address,
and anything else a person or a model wrote. A span's name, its tags, a
user id and an error's type (its class, never its message) are metadata
you choose to send, and the content mode does not decide what happens to
them. Everyone on the project sees the mode under **Content** on the
project's **Settings** page (`/{organization}/{project}/settings`).

- **Off**, the mode a project starts in unless its Owner chose **On** when
  creating it, keeps none of it: each piece of
  content is dropped as its span arrives, and the span is kept without it.
- **On** keeps it as it arrives, wherever the code asks the SDK to send it
  too: sealed under a key of the project's own, in Postgres alone, for as long
  as its span — every message, tool payload, image and file a message carried,
  a failure's message, stack trace and events, and the metadata a team
  attaches — and the trace page shows it in full
  ([Traces](/docs/monitoring/traces#content), [Content](/docs/monitoring/content)).
  Turning it back to **Off** takes in no more, and keeps what was sent until
  its span's retention ends, unless the Owner also ticks **Also delete what
  this project kept**
  ([Deleting a project's content](/docs/workspace/data-deletion#deleting-a-projects-content)).
- Only the organization's **Owner** can change a project's mode, because what
  a project keeps changes what the organization is responsible for. Admins and
  Members see the mode and cannot change it.
- Every change is recorded in the [audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log), and
  so is each read of the content, by a person, an export or an API key, before it is
  shown. The console agent reads none of it, in any mode.

Ingest reads the mode on every export, so it acts at the door, as the span
arrives. The project's **Settings** page shows the mode; the [field
inventory](/docs/api/field-inventory) lists every attribute ingest reads, and
what becomes of each.

Project **Settings** also shows **Door scrubbers**: owners and admins can
choose whether foreign exporters' text is scrubbed for secrets (on by default)
and personal data (off by default). Each change is audited and applies to
subsequent exports. SDK exports keep the developer's scrubber policy.
See [Content](/docs/monitoring/content).

### Deleting a project

Only the **Owner** can delete a project. Deletion is initiated from the
**Danger zone** on the project's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/{project}/settings`):

- The dialog asks you to type the project's name before it deletes anything.
- It takes effect at once, with no undo.
- Deleting a project deletes its API keys, member seats, and pending
  invitations with it, and its connectors, their delivery history, and its
  activity feed are purged right after (whatever fails then is tried again
  every hour until it goes). The console agent removes what it holds about
  the project.
- Its traces go with it: every span and every hour of totals the project
  sent is marked deleted in the same purge, gone from every read within
  moments as the store applies the mark, and the nightly rotation removes
  them from disk. Telemetry is kept only while
  its project is active, never past its deletion.
- [Data deletion](/docs/workspace/data-deletion) lists everything a deletion
  removes, when, and what stays.
- Historical audit events for that project remain preserved in the organization's
  append-only audit chain.

### Transferring a project

An organization Owner can hand a project to one of the project's Admins:

1. The Owner selects **Transfer project** on the Admin's row on the project's
   **Members** page (`/{organization}/{project}/members`) and confirms with
   **Send offer**. Only someone with an Admin seat on the project can be
   offered it, so invite an organization Admin to the project as an Admin
   first.
2. The Admin is emailed, and the offer appears in their console bell and under
   **Project offers** on their account's **Notifications** page
   (`/settings/notifications`). It stands for **7 days**, and a project has one
   live offer at a time: a new offer withdraws the earlier one, and whoever
   held it is told. The Owner can withdraw it with **Withdraw offer** on the
   same row; removing the Admin from the project, or their leaving it, ends the
   offer too. The Owner is emailed a decline, and the Admin a withdrawal.
3. The Admin accepts the project into an organization they own. The page asks
   which one when they own several, and offers only **Decline** when they own
   none.
4. On acceptance:
   - The project row moves into the new organization. It keeps its name unless
     that organization already has a project by it, in which case it arrives
     with the next free number on the end (`Payments 2`). Its URL changes with
     the move (see [Name and URL](#name-and-url)).
   - Its API keys follow into the new organization. The offer on the
     Notifications page advises the new owner to rotate any key they did not
     mint.
   - Its [content mode](#a-projects-content-mode) follows it, for the new
     organization's Owner to change.
   - Project seats and pending invitations stay on the project, and everyone
     seated is enrolled on the new organization's roster as a **Member**, as a
     project invitation would enrol them — a seat given from the old
     organization's roster among them, and one its holder had not yet seen
     leaves their bell with the move. The accepting Admin's own seat is
     folded into their ownership.
   - The former owner stays on the project as an **Admin**, joins the new
     organization as a **Member**, and is emailed that the project moved.
   - **Connectors do not follow**: a project's Slack grants and webhook
     secrets are the old organization's, so they are removed before
     the move commits, with their delivery history and the project's activity
     feed, and a removal that fails moves nothing. The console agent removes
     what it holds about the project in the old organization.
   - **Telemetry and content move with it**: every span, and the
     [content](#a-projects-content-mode) the project keeps, which still opens
     and which [erasing an end user](/docs/workspace/data-deletion) still
     finds, its size counted on the new organization from the move. Each
     earlier read of that content stays in the old organization's log, with
     the project's other earlier events.
   - Both organizations' audit logs record the move; the receiving one also
     notes the name the project was offered under when a clash renamed it. The
     project's earlier events stay in the old organization's log, so its
     **Audit log** page starts at the move.

## Organizations

### Creating an organization

Anyone signed in to the console can create another organization, and becomes
its **Owner**:

1. Open the switcher in the console header and select **New organization**;
   a panel opens over the page, as **New project** does.
2. Give it a **Name**: up to 80 characters, the name everyone in it sees.
3. Give it a **URL**, or leave the field blank to have one derived from the
   name, as [Name and URL](#name-and-url) describes: "Acme Robotics" becomes
   `acme-robotics`, with six random characters on the end when another
   organization holds that, and a name with no Latin letter or digit gets a placeholder (`org-`
   and ten random characters) until you choose a URL. A URL you give must be
   a slug no other organization holds, not one of the console's own words, and
   holding no profanity or slur.
4. Select **Create organization**. The console opens the new organization's
   **Projects** page; [create its first project](#creating-a-project) there
   or from the switcher. On the hosted service, a second
   organization is paid for first, as below: the panel asks for its plan and
   sends you to the payment page, and the organization opens once the
   payment is confirmed.

A deployment may hold each person to a number of organizations they own,
whatever their plans: past it, creating one, or accepting an organization's
ownership, is refused, and the console names who to write to for more. On the
hosted service the number is 10
([Limits](https://telmoni.com/docs/workspace/billing#limits)); a self-hosted
deployment sets none. Being a member of other people's organizations is never
limited. Creating one leaves your [default organization](/docs/settings/account#default-organization),
the one a sign-in opens, as it was. An operator who has [closed new
organizations](/docs/self-host/production#closing-new-organizations) closes this
too: the switcher offers no **New organization**. On the hosted service, one you
create while you already own an organization on its free plan is paid for
before it exists, on Pro or Teams, and created once the payment is confirmed
([One Hobby organization](https://telmoni.com/docs/workspace/billing#one-hobby-organization)).

### Renaming an organization

An Owner or Admin can rename an organization from its **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/settings`). The name can be up to 80 characters. A rename
moves nothing: the organization's URL is its own setting on the same page (see
[Name and URL](#name-and-url)).

### Transferring organization ownership

An organization has exactly one Owner. The Owner can transfer ownership to any
organization Admin; on the hosted service, one on the free plan goes only to an
Admin who owns no organization on it
([One Hobby organization](https://telmoni.com/docs/workspace/billing#one-hobby-organization)):

1. The Owner selects **Transfer ownership** on the Admin's row on the
   organization's **Members** page (`/{organization}/members`) and confirms
   with **Send offer**.
2. The Admin is emailed, and the offer appears in their console bell and on
   their account's **Notifications** page (`/settings/notifications`). It stands
   for **7 days**, and an organization has one live offer at a time: a new
   offer withdraws the earlier one, and whoever held it is told. The Owner may
   withdraw the offer at any time before it is accepted; removing the Admin
   from the organization, or their leaving it, ends the offer too.
3. The Admin accepts (confirming with **Become the owner**) or declines on the
   Notifications page. The Owner is emailed either answer, and the Admin is
   emailed a withdrawal.
4. Upon acceptance:
   - The accepting Admin becomes the new **Owner**. Any project seats they held
     are removed, since the Owner's standing on every project comes from the
     organization.
   - The former owner becomes an **Admin** in the organization, and may then
     leave it.
   - All projects, API keys, connectors, and audit histories stay intact. The
     handover, and each removed seat, is written to the audit log.

### Dividing its limits among its projects

Where a deployment sells plans, a plan's spans, content, alerts and alert
deliveries are the organization's, one pool every project draws on. Its
**Limits** page, in the organization's rail, shows each project you can open
beside what it has used this month: spans, content held, alerts, monitors,
objectives, and alert deliveries today where capped. Every member reads it.

The organization's Owner and Admins may cap a project at less than the plan
holds, so one busy project cannot use what the others need: **Set caps** on
its row takes a number for each, at most the plan's, and an empty one shares
the pool. Every change is in the [audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log). Past
its cap, that project alone is held as the organization is past its plan:

- **Spans** past the month's cap are sampled, one trace in ten kept and flagged,
  with no grace, since the cap is the team's own. A cap set partway through
  the month counts what the project sent in its earlier hours, though not in
  the hour under way when it was set.
- **Content** past its cap is dropped as it arrives, its spans keeping their
  metadata.
- **A new alert, monitor or objective** is refused, naming the cap and who may
  raise it, as is an edit that turns an alert into an objective or an objective
  into an alert past the cap of what it becomes, and a
  [starter alert](/docs/monitoring/alerts) is not made.
- **Alert deliveries** past the day's cap reach the feed alone until midnight
  UTC.

The organization's Owner and Admins are told once in the feed when a project's
spans, the content its spans carry or its alert deliveries pass a cap; a new
alert, monitor or objective refused at its cap is told only to the member who
asked. The organization's other projects carry on. A project's own **Settings**
page shows its caps to its members. A project moved to another organization
leaves its caps behind, and finds them again if it moves back.

### Deleting an organization

Only the **Owner** can delete an organization. Deletion is performed from the
**Danger zone** in the organization's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/settings`):

1. Request a 6-digit confirmation code, which is emailed to the Owner's address
   and remains valid for 15 minutes. Five wrong guesses burn it; **Resend
   code** issues another. The console sends at most three codes an hour and
   takes ten attempts an hour. On a self-hosted deployment, the code goes out
   only when the operator has configured mail (`SMTP_URL`); without it, the
   organization cannot be deleted from the console.
2. Enter the confirmation code.
3. **Access ends immediately**: the organization is closed, all API keys stop
   authenticating, and member access ceases. On the hosted service, its
   billing is closed at once; a part that cannot finish is retried every ten
   minutes, and the confirmation screen says so when that happens. Your own
   account is untouched, and if this was your last organization, your next
   console page provisions a new one while new organizations are allowed, as
   your [first
   sign-in](/docs/getting-started/sign-in#what-your-first-sign-in-creates) did.
4. **Erasure follows, usually within the hour**: the automated deletion sweep
   permanently purges the organization, its projects and their traces, API
   keys, connectors, notices and console agent data. There is no restore
   window and no undo. [Data deletion](/docs/workspace/data-deletion) lists
   everything it removes, and what stays.

An organization an operator closed from the server (`telmoni-server terminate`, on a
self-hosted instance) goes the same way.

## Leaving an organization or project

- **Leaving an organization**: Anyone but the Owner can leave from the
  organization's **Projects** page (`/{organization}/projects`), with **Leave
  organization**. Leaving removes you from the organization and from every
  project in it.
- **Leaving a project**: Your own row of the project's **Members** page
  (`/{organization}/{project}/members`) offers **Leave**, whatever your seat's
  role and however it came, by invitation or from the organization's roster.
  Leaving gives up the seat alone: you stay on the organization's roster, and
  an Owner or Admin can add you back. The organization's **Projects** page
  (`/{organization}/projects`) offers an Admin **Leave** beside each project they still hold a
  seat on; they keep their access to every project through their organization
  role, and the Owner, who holds every project outright, is offered none.
- The **Owner cannot leave** an active organization. To leave, the Owner must
  either transfer ownership to an Admin or delete the organization.
