title: "Data deletion"
description: "What deleting a project, an organization, a trace, an end user, your account, an API key or a connector removes, how fast, the receipt each leaves, what stays — the audit log, and copies already delivered elsewhere — and what cannot be deleted on its own yet."
---

Deletion in Telmoni is final: there is no restore window and no undo, and
every deletion takes its telemetry with it, since telemetry is kept only
while its project is active. This page says what each deletion removes and
when; the steps are on the pages it links to. How long each kind of data is
kept otherwise is on [Data retention](/docs/workspace/data-retention).

## Deleting a project

Only the organization's **Owner** can, from the project's **Settings** page,
after typing the project's name
([steps](/docs/workspace/organizations-and-projects#deleting-a-project)).

- **At once:** the project is gone from the console, its API keys stop
  authenticating, and its member seats and pending invitations go with it.
- **In the same purge:** every span and every hour of totals the project
  sent is marked deleted, gone from every read within moments as the store
  applies the mark — an export still being written as the project went
  marked once it lands — and the nightly rotation removes them from disk; its
  telemetry settings, its saved dashboards, its alerts with their
  incidents and its maintenance windows, the content it kept, and the keys
  its end-user and session ids were hashed and its content sealed under go with them. Its connectors, their delivery history and
  its activity feed are purged in the same request, just before its
  telemetry — the Slack app uninstalled right after from every workspace no
  other connection uses — and the console agent removes what it holds about
  the project within the hour.
- **What stays:** the project's events in the organization's audit log.
- **Receipt:** the deletion is in the audit log at once, and whoever deleted
  the project is mailed once its telemetry's purge lands, the mail saying
  since when its content has been unreadable (*Backups and copies*, below).
- **If a step fails:** its telemetry's purge — cut short by a store that
  did not answer, or by the project's first export still filing its
  settings past the purge's two seconds — is tried again every ten minutes
  until it lands, telemetry finding the project through the settings row the
  failure left; its connectors' purge — its connectors
  first, then its feed in chunks, so one cut short by a database that did not
  answer, or by a feed too large for the request's time, keeps what it did —
  is tried again every hour until it lands, and an uninstall Slack did not
  answer is asked again every hour for a day.

## Deleting an organization

Only the **Owner** can, with a confirmation code sent to their address
([steps](/docs/workspace/organizations-and-projects#deleting-an-organization)).

- **At once:** the organization is closed: every API key stops
  authenticating, and member access ends.
- **After a 15-minute grace**, the deletion sweep, which runs every ten
  minutes, erases it: its projects, with everything a project's deletion
  removes above; memberships and pending invitations; API keys; connectors
  and the notices queued for them; the audit log's exports; the console
  agent's conversations and search index; and telemetry's settings, saved
  views, alerts, kept content and keys. Usually within the hour. A part that cannot finish is
  retried every ten minutes until it does.
- **Receipt:** the request is in the audit log at once, and the owner who
  asked is mailed once the sweep has erased it, the mail saying since when
  its content has been unreadable; a closing by an operator mails nobody.
- **What stays:** the audit log, which is append-only and outlives the
  organization; and copies already delivered
  to a Slack channel or a webhook the organization connected, which are the
  organization's.
- On a self-hosted deployment, an operator can close an organization from
  the server with `telmoni-server terminate`, which goes the same way.

## Deleting a trace or an end user

A project's **Admin** or the organization's **Owner** can.

- **A trace:** **Delete** on the trace's page.
- **An end user:** **Erase an end user** on the project's **Settings** page,
  by the id your app sends as `user.id`, matched exactly as it was sent:
  every trace whose spans carry it, each whole, since a trace's other spans
  need not carry the id, and every trace of one of their sessions that names
  no user. A trace naming someone else stays, even in a session they share.
- **At once:** the traces' spans are gone from every read before the
  deletion answers, and the content they kept is deleted; the nightly
  rotation removes the spans from disk. A trace still running is remembered
  for a day, and what it sends after is dropped as it arrives.
- **Receipt:** the request is recorded in the organization's audit log
  before anything goes — a trace by its run's id, an end user by the keyed
  hash their id is stored as, never the id — and you are mailed once it is
  done.
- **What stays:** the hourly totals, counts and costs that name no trace or
  user, keep counting them; an alert's incident keeps the run's id, which
  then opens nothing.
- **If a step fails:** the answer is an error, and trying again finishes the
  deletion; the audit log then records each request.

## Deleting your account

From your account's **Privacy** page, with a confirmation code, once you own
no organization that has other members
([steps and the full list](/docs/settings/privacy#deleting-your-account)).

- **Erased:** your sessions, your memberships, the audit log exports you
  started, your conversations with the console agent and your own index
  rows, your linked Slack accounts, and your record at the identity provider
  on the hosted service.
- **Deleted with you:** every organization you own alone, after the same
  15-minute grace.
- **Rewritten:** feed notices and other people's agent answers that named
  you say "a former member" instead.
- **Kept, naming nobody:** your account's id on the incidents you
  acknowledged or resolved and the maintenance windows you set or ended on a
  project's [Alerts](/docs/monitoring/alerts) page, which no roster names once
  you are gone.

## Deleting a project's content

Only the organization's **Owner** can, on the project's **Settings** page, as
its content mode goes back to `off`: choosing **Off**, they may tick **Also
delete what this project kept**. Leaving it unticked keeps what the project
kept readable, each read in the audit log, until its spans' retention ends.

- **At once:** the key the content was sealed under is deleted in the same
  change as the mode, so nothing reads it from then; a backup taken before
  holds it until it expires (*Backups and copies*, below).
- **Right after:** its rows are purged, and their bytes come off the
  organization's count.
- **Receipt:** the change is in the audit log, and the owner is mailed a
  receipt saying since when the content has been unreadable. Its spans and
  their metadata stay until their retention ends.

## Deleting smaller things

- **An API key:** **Revoke** ends it at once, and its record is deleted seven
  days later. **Rotate** keeps the old key working for a day while you deploy
  the new one ([API keys](/docs/api/api-keys)).
- **A connector:** disconnecting it erases the grant or signing secret, and
  its deliveries and their attempts go with it; a Slack app no other
  connection uses is uninstalled from the workspace, its bot token kept,
  sealed, until Slack has answered, a day at most
  ([Notifications](/docs/integrations/notifications)).
- **A session:** end any session but the current one from **Active
  sessions**, or every other at once with **Sign out everywhere else**; the
  device signs out within minutes
  ([Privacy](/docs/settings/privacy#active-sessions)).
- **An invitation:** withdraw it; its record goes 30 days later.

## What cannot be deleted on its own yet

- **A session's telemetry, alone.** It goes with its traces, its end
  user's erasure, its project, or when its day passes the project's retention.
- **An audit log entry.** The audit log is append-only by design, kept
  through every deletion, and verified daily; only an export's file is a
  copy, and it is deleted after seven days.

## Backups and copies

On the hosted service, deleted data leaves the backups as they expire: the
database keeps thirty daily backups and telemetry's store nightly ones, and
nothing older than thirty days is kept of either.

When a project or its organization is deleted, or a project's content is
deleted as its mode goes `off`, the content's key goes in the same commit:
each field is sealed under a key of the project's own, so from that moment
nothing reads the content and no backup taken after holds it, the moment the
receipt gives as "unreadable since". A backup taken before holds the key
beside the content, and leaves as it expires, as everything else does; so
does a deleted trace's or end user's content, whose project's key stays for
its other content. On a self-hosted deployment, your backups are yours. Files
you downloaded and notices already delivered to your channels are yours too,
and nothing recalls them.
