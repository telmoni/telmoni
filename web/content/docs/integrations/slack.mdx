A Slack connector posts project [notifications](/docs/integrations/notifications)
directly into a specified channel.

Connectors are configured per project. Project **Owners** and **Admins** can
connect and manage destinations from the project's **Connectors** page
(`/{organization}/{project}/connectors`). Project **Members** can view the
project's connectors and their delivery histories.

## Connecting a channel

1. Open your project's **Connectors** page.
2. Select **Connect Slack**.
3. You will be directed to Slack's authorization screen. Choose the
   destination workspace and target channel.
4. Once authorized, you are redirected back to the **Connectors** page, where
   the channel shows as **Connected**. Connecting raises a `connector_connected`
   notice, which the channel receives as its first message.

Authorization handshakes must be completed within 10 minutes. An install
Slack approved that Telmoni then refuses — the project moved to another
organization, or your role changed, while you were at Slack — creates no
connection: the app is uninstalled from the workspace again unless another
connection on the platform uses it there, its bot token kept, sealed, until
Slack has answered, a day at most, as after a disconnect.

**Send test** on a connected channel posts one message, titled "Slack is
connected", that reads "This is a test from Telmoni. Notices for this project
will arrive here." The test is not recorded in the project's
[audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log) or its delivery log.

Slack shows each notice as a header with its title, its body, and its kind
below them. An incident's opening, and each reminder of it, also carries its
buttons ([acting from Slack](#acting-from-slack)).

On a self-hosted deployment whose operator has not configured the Slack app,
the Slack tile shows **Soon**, with **Connect** turned off (see
[The Slack app](/docs/self-host/configuration#the-slack-app)).

## Acting from Slack

The notice of an incident's opening, and each reminder of it, carries three
buttons:

- **Acknowledge** takes the incident, as **Acknowledge** on the project's
  [Alerts page](/docs/monitoring/alerts) does: its reminders stop.
- **Mute for an hour** makes a [maintenance window](/docs/monitoring/alerts)
  on the incident's alert, from now to an hour from now: the incident stays in
  the feed, and no Slack channel or webhook hears of the alert until the window
  ends. Where a window already mutes the alert, none is made, and you are told
  until when.
- **Open the run** opens the run the notice shows, in the console. It appears
  only on a notice that shows a run, and acts on nothing.

A press acts as the Telmoni member whose
[linked Slack account](/docs/settings/account#slack) pressed it, with the role
they hold on the incident's project now. Telmoni matches the press by the Slack
workspace and user IDs alone, never by an address. Only you see the answer, in
the channel:

- Pressed from a Slack account nobody linked, the button acts on nothing, and
  the answer links to your account settings to link yours.
- Pressed by somebody with no role on the incident's project, it acts on
  nothing and says so; anyone in a shared channel is held to the same.
- An incident already acknowledged or resolved is said to be so, and nothing
  more is recorded.

A press that acts is recorded in the project's
[audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log) exactly as the console records the same
action, by the member it acted as.
Slack signs every press, and Telmoni refuses one whose signature does not
verify, or that is more than five minutes old.

## Connection states

The Connectors page shows each channel in one of three states:

- **Connected**: notices are posted as they arrive.
- **Needs attention**: the installation stands, but the channel refuses posts:
  it was archived or deleted, or Slack refuses posting into the workspace's
  general channel. Notices queued for it fail.
- **Disconnected**: the installation itself is gone: the Telmoni app was
  removed from the Slack workspace or its token revoked. Notices queued for it
  fail.

In either failed state, **Reconnect** on the row runs Slack's authorization
again. Picking the same channel reconnects it in place, and the audit log notes
the reconnection; picking another channel makes a new connection. No notice is
queued for a connection while it is in a failed state, so reconnecting does not
deliver the notices raised meanwhile.

## Permissions and security

Telmoni requests one scope from Slack, the one that lets it post into the
channel you pick: `incoming-webhook`. It requests no scope for reading
messages, listing channels, or listing workspace members. The grant and the
webhook target it receives are stored encrypted at rest. Linking a person's own
Slack account asks that person for `openid` alone, through Sign in with Slack,
and keeps no token from it.

## Delivery and retries

A notice's first attempt starts as soon as the notice is written, unless 32
first attempts are already in flight on that server; then the delivery loop
takes it on its next poll. If a delivery attempt fails due to a transient
network error, a rate limit, a server error on Slack's side, or a `408`
timeout:

- Telmoni makes up to **5 attempts** in all: after 30 seconds, then 2, 8, and 32
  minutes.
- If Slack answers with a `Retry-After` header, Telmoni waits at least that
  long before the next attempt, up to a day.

A refusal that says the channel or the installation is gone moves the
connection to **Needs attention** or **Disconnected**, as the
[states](#connection-states) above describe. So does a disabled workspace, a
revoked token, or an app no longer allowed to post there. Any other refusal
fails that notice at once, without retries, and leaves the connection
**Connected**.

## Disconnection

If Slack answers that the destination is gone (the channel was deleted or
archived, the incoming webhook was removed, or the Telmoni app was uninstalled
from the workspace), Telmoni marks the connector **Needs attention** or
**Disconnected** as the [states](#connection-states) above describe, fails the
notices still queued for it, and posts a `connector_disconnected` notice to the
project's other connectors (see
[where you read notices](/docs/integrations/notifications#where-you-read-them)).

Removing the Telmoni app from a Slack workspace disconnects all channels
connected to that Slack workspace. The event is recorded in the organization's
[audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log) with `external:slack` as the actor.

## Disconnecting manually

To remove a connection, select **Disconnect** on the connector's row on the
**Connectors** page. Telmoni deletes the connection with its queue and delivery
log, records the action in the project's audit log, and uninstalls the Slack
app from the workspace once no other connection on the platform uses it,
asking again every hour for a day where Slack does not answer.
