An **alert** watches a measure of a project's spans over the last hour, day
or week against an alert threshold and, if you give one, a warning threshold
met first: the share of traces that failed, the p95 duration of model calls,
the tokens they used, the most a trace spent, the most times a trace called one
tool in a row, the traces left open, the average of a
[score](/docs/monitoring/scores) — or its change from the window before, so a
drop opens an incident. It is evaluated every minute, a
[starter alert](#starter-alerts) every five.
When its value crosses a threshold it opens an **incident**, told in the
project's feed and to the connectors the alert links; when the value comes
back inside its thresholds the incident resolves, and that is told too. A
project's **Alerts** page (`/{organization}/{project}/alerts`) holds both,
each alert's incidents under it. Everything on it is metadata — names and
numbers, never a prompt, a completion or a tool payload — and so is every
notice.

A project with no alert opens **Alerts** on a guide: where its alerts go
first, Slack or a webhook on the **Connectors** page, ticked once the project
has one, and then what to watch: the three [starter alerts](#starter-alerts)
its first trace makes, and more with **New alert**.

Any member of the project, whatever their role, makes, edits, pauses, mutes
and deletes its alerts, and acknowledges and resolves its incidents, each
change recorded in the organization's [audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log).

## The list

Every alert of the project, by name, each with where it stands, **Starter**
on a [starter alert](#starter-alerts), its controls, and its metric in its
notices' own words — "The p95 duration of model calls over the last hour" —
then:

| Line | What it says |
|---|---|
| Now | Its last value and when it was read, and since when it has stood at its severity; **Not evaluated** until its first evaluation, within a minute of being made or resumed; **No severity**, and when it was read, where it keeps its last severity and its window has held no span yet, so it has none to keep; saved over, that it is read again within a minute, its severity standing until then; **Paused**, and since when |
| Thresholds | The alert threshold, and the warning one, as its operator compares them |
| Filters | The values its spans must carry, field by field; **None** reads every span of its kind |
| No data | What a window holding no span means |
| Notified again | How often a breach held is told again |
| Connectors | Each connector its notices go to, whether it hears warnings and alerts or alerts alone, and in which hours. One that hears nothing, or only part, says so: one that **Needs attention** or is **Disconnected** on the Connectors page; a webhook whose events take neither of an incident's kinds, **Takes no incidents**, or one alone, **Takes openings only** or **Takes resolutions only** ([choosing events](/docs/integrations/webhooks#choosing-events)); and one whose last five deliveries of this alert failed, which hears no more of it until **Hear again** turns it back on |
| Maintenance | The windows that mute it, its own and every alert's, in force or still to come, each with **End** |
| Incidents | The [incidents](#incidents) it opened |

**Edit** opens the alert's form filled in. **Pause** stops its evaluation
until a member **Resume**s it: a paused alert opens and tells nothing, and an
incident it holds open stays open until a member resolves it, or the alert,
resumed, recovers. **Mute** opens a
[maintenance window](#maintenance-windows) for it. **Delete** removes it at
once, with its incidents and its maintenance windows; an incident still open
is first told resolved, as a member's resolution is, to the connectors that
heard it.

An [objective](/docs/monitoring/objectives) is listed here too, as the alert
on its burn it is: its metric says what it promises, its thresholds the burns
that page and warn, and its incidents stand under it. **Edit** opens the
objective's own form.

On a deployment that sells plans, such as the hosted service, an
organization holds as many alerts across its projects as its plan does,
paused ones, objectives and starters counted, and its alerts make as many deliveries a UTC day as its
plan holds, the feed never counted. An alert past the number is refused when
it is made. Where the organization holds more than its plan does, the oldest
alerts keep running and the newest wait, **Paused by the plan**, until there
is room, and resuming one is refused while there is none. **Keep paused** on
one makes it a member's pause, so it stays paused once there is room. Once the
day's
deliveries are made, its incidents reach the feed alone until midnight UTC,
and the organization's Owner and Admins are told once that day. A
self-hosted deployment holds no number.

## Incidents

Under each alert, the incidents it opened, those not resolved first, then the
newest; the page reads the project's 200 at most, so an alert's oldest
resolved ones may not show. Each says:

| Line | What it says |
|---|---|
| Severity | **Alert**, **Warning**, or **No data** where the alert asks to be told of a window holding no span |
| Status | **Open**; **Acknowledged**, by whom and when; **Resolved** by a member, or **Recovered** where its alert came back inside its thresholds, and when |
| Opened | When it opened, and how often it reopened |
| Told | Where its notices went: the feed, then each connector they were sent to; one the project has removed since says so |
| Trace | The trace its last notice showed, a link to it |
| Changed | What the alert's agents [changed to](#what-changed-before-it) in the 24 hours before it opened, each a link to the trace that first carried it; nothing where nothing changed |

**Acknowledge** takes an open incident, and its reminders stop. **Resolve**
ends it by hand, told to its alert's connectors as a recovery is; an alert
still past its threshold opens no new incident until it recovers and breaches
again, or rises from the warning a member resolved to an alert, which opens
it again. A Slack channel told of an incident offers **Acknowledge** on its
notice too, beside **Mute for an hour**, a maintenance window over its alert
([acting from Slack](/docs/integrations/slack#acting-from-slack)).

An alert holds one incident at a time. A breach within an hour of its
recovery reopens it rather than opening another, so an alert that flaps
opens one incident an hour at most, and a rise from warning to alert follows
the incident it is in. What each notice says is on
[Notifications](/docs/integrations/notifications#notice-types).

### Fault categories

**Faults** measures spans with one of the categories you select: rate limited,
overloaded, timeout, context length, unauthorized, invalid output, refused,
truncated or other. Telmoni derives the class from `error.type`; an unknown
failure is **Other**. A successful model call can carry **Refused** or
**Truncated** from its finish reasons. Cancellation carries no fault, and a
fault does not change a span's status or an existing **Failures** alert.

Choose **How many with selected faults** or **Share with selected faults**.
The share divides matching spans by every span of the selected kind admitted
by the ordinary filters, including successful calls. Several categories count
each span once. With **Reads: Model calls**, **Faults: Rate limited**, a provider
filter, **Window: Last hour** and **Alert when: > 5%**, one rate-limited call
among twenty is exactly 5% and does not breach; two among twenty do. Thresholds
are typed as percentages. A window with no calls follows **No data** as configured.

Fault alerts use the same warnings, incidents, recovery and connector delivery
as other alerts. Their example trace contains a matching fault, and their
Traces link applies the selected categories.

### What changed before it

An incident names each [change](/docs/monitoring/traces#changes) its alert's
agents made in the 24 hours before it opened: a release, a model or a
prompt's version their traces had not carried, and how long before, as
*release 2026.11.3 in production, 14 minutes before*. The newest stands
first, 20 at most, and the incident says where there were more. Nothing is
inferred: Telmoni says what changed and when, and whether it broke anything
is yours to judge.

- **Whose changes.** Those of the agents the alert's **Agent** filter names,
  in the environments its **Environment** filter names; an objective's are
  its agent's. An alert that names no agent, as every starter does, reads
  every agent's, and each line then names its agent. The filters are read as
  they stand: an alert edited since its incident opened names the changes of
  the agents it reads now.
- **The window is the opening's.** A change at the minute the incident opened
  is named, and one after it is not. A reopening keeps the first opening's
  window, so a release made after it and before the reopening is not named.
- **Read as you look.** The page reads the changes each time, so one found
  after the incident opened, as another exporter's is once its trace lands,
  is named here though the incident's notice went without it. Where they
  cannot be read, the incident says **Changes couldn't be read**, which is
  not that nothing changed.
- **Only what is marked.** A change past its agent's 24 of a kind in a UTC
  day is marked nowhere, so it is not named here either; the 24th says its
  day marked no more.
- **As long as its traces are kept.** A change goes with its trace's day once
  the project's [retention](/docs/workspace/data-retention) passes it, so an
  old incident names fewer.

The opening's notice names the newest five the same way
([Notifications](/docs/integrations/notifications#notice-types)), and
[`GET /v1/incidents`](/docs/api/reference#get-v1incidents) answers them as
`changes`.

## Making an alert

**New alert** opens the form over the page. On a project with no connector it
asks first: **Add a connector** leads its owners and admins to the Connectors
page, and **Continue with the feed** makes an alert whose incidents reach the
project's feed alone.

| Field | What it takes |
|---|---|
| Name | 1 to 60 characters; a project holds one alert of a name, whatever its case |
| Reads | Traces, steps, model calls, tool calls or retrievals |
| Measure | **Count**, how many there were; **Failures**, those that ended in error; **Faults**, those with a [selected class](#fault-categories); **Duration**; **Tokens**, on model calls; **Cost**, on traces: what each trace's model calls cost, read from the calls as they land, so an alert on it tells while the trace is still going ([what a call cost](/docs/api/ingest#what-a-call-cost)); **Steps**, on traces: each trace's steps; **Repeats**, on traces: the most times in a row each trace called one tool; **Stalled**, on traces: the traces left open ([loops and stalls](#loops-and-stalls)); **Score**, on traces: every [score](/docs/monitoring/scores) of the name you give, 1 to 64 characters, the project holds — your code's and its members' — a number's value, a yes as 1 and a no as 0, each once as it stands, counted in the window its last change landed in — a score given again or taken back moves to then — one taken back left out and a category never read |
| Folded as | How the measure is read over the window: a count; failures as how many or as the share that failed; a duration's p95, p99, p90, p50, average or total; tokens' average or total; a trace's cost as the most a trace spent — an alert tells, and the SDKs' [guard](/docs/api/sdks#the-guard) is what stops a trace at its cap — the average a trace spent, or what the traces spent in all; a trace's steps as the most a trace took, their p95, p99, p90, p50 or average; repeats as the most; stalled traces as how many; and scores' average, p50, p90, p95, p99, highest, or how many |
| Window | The last hour, day or week, or the calendar month so far, from its first at midnight UTC; **Stalled** takes the last hour, day or week alone |
| Read as | Its value; or its change from the window of the same length before it, in percent of that window's value, so a drop is below 0 — the average relevance score down a fifth on the day before is −20% — and none where either window held nothing, or the one before 0; a count of an empty window is 0, so a stream that stops reads as −100%. Not on **This month**, which has no window of its length before it |
| Filters | Agent, environment and release on every kind; model and provider on model calls, and on a trace's cost, read from its calls; tag, each `key:value`, on a trace's cost alone, a trace passing where any of its spans carries it; tool on tool calls; source on retrievals, the vector store or memory each searched; and the status a span ended with, on all but a cost, steps, repeats and stalls, which are read before a trace has a status of its own. None on a score, which names no agent, model or status of its own. Separate a field's values with commas, and put a value that holds one between double quotes: a span passes a field it carries any of the values of, and passes the alert when it passes every field filled |
| Compared as | How the value is compared: above, at or above, below, at or below, equal to, or not equal to |
| Alert when | The alert threshold under that comparison: in milliseconds for a duration, as a percentage for the share failed, in US dollars for a cost, and as a percentage for a change, below 0 for a drop: **Below** −20 alerts on a fall of more than a fifth |
| From its history | Where the alert reads one agent, or every agent, and filters by nothing else: the 50th, 90th and 99th percentiles of what that agent usually does, read from the hourly totals of the last 14 whole UTC days, so they change once a night. For a duration, its spans' own durations; for tokens folded as an average, an hour's tokens a model call, and as a total over the last hour, an hour's tokens; for a trace's cost totalled over the last hour, an hour's spend; and for failures folded as the share that failed over the last hour, the share of an hour's spans that failed, in whole percents up. Each percentile is a button that puts it in **Alert when**, and the line under them says what it replaces |
| Warn when | A warning threshold under the same comparison, met before the alert's: below it for above, above it for below. Optional, and none for equal to or not equal to |
| No data | A window holding nothing to read is read as 0; keeps the alert's last severity; is shown as **No data**; or is shown so and told once a whole window has passed with none. A window of no score, and a change with nothing to compare, is no 0, so neither takes **Read as 0** unless the alert counts |
| Notify again | Every 1 to 10,080 minutes while an incident holds at one severity and nobody has acknowledged it; empty for never |
| Connectors | The project's Slack channels and webhooks it tells, up to 20, each hearing its warnings and alerts or its alerts alone, at any hour or in set hours: days, a start and an end, at an offset from UTC that stays put, so a change of the clocks moves the hours by its own. An end before the start runs past midnight; the same time at both ends is the whole day. A connector out of its hours when an incident was told hears it once they begin, if it is still open and not acknowledged, and one that heard an incident hears it resolve at any hour. An incident of **No data** reaches only those hearing warnings and alerts |

An alert that links no connector tells the project's feed alone, and a
webhook hears an alert's incidents only if it takes `incident_opened` and
`incident_resolved` ([choosing events](/docs/integrations/webhooks#choosing-events)).
Saving an alert over forgets its last value, which answered another
question, and it is evaluated again within a minute.

### A budget

A budget is an alert on what the traces spent in all over **This month**: on
traces, measured as **Cost**, folded as what the traces spent, its alert
threshold the budget and its warning threshold where you want to hear first,
80% of it say, for the project or, with a tag filter, for the traces one team's
tag names, each trace counted as the traces its [sample
rate](/docs/api/ingest#what-is-dropped-at-the-door) stands for where a plan
sampled it. Without a tag it reads the month
from the hourly totals, which outlive a retention shorter than a month. Its
warning says where the month is headed at its burn so far: the
day it reaches the alert threshold, or that it stays under it this month. A
budget opens its month again on the first, at midnight UTC.

### Loops and stalls

An alert on **Repeats** reads each trace's tool calls in the order they started
and counts the longest stretch of one tool's name called back to back.
Whatever the trace does between two of them, a model call or a step, breaks no
stretch, so an agent that asks its model and calls `search` again, twelve
times over, is a stretch of 12; calls that start at the same moment, as a
model's parallel tool calls do, count as back to back. Folded as the most, its
alert threshold is the stretch you call a loop. An alert on **Steps** counts
each trace's steps. Both read every trace with a span in the window whole, from a
day before the window on, and as its spans land, so a trace still going is read
as far as it has gone, and one that loops tells before it ends.

An alert on **Stalled** counts the traces whose spans have landed and whose
agent's own span, which the SDK sends as the trace ends, has not, quiet longer
than the window: on the last hour, each trace whose last span landed one to
two hours ago and that never ended. The SDKs send a trace's opening as it
opens, so a trace that stops before any step of it lands still counts. A trace
is counted so for one window, then left out, so the
alert recovers once its stalls are that old, or as soon as their own spans
land. A trace that waits longer than the window, on a person say, counts too:
choose a window longer than your traces wait. Traces from another exporter
([frameworks](/docs/api/frameworks)) land whole, their agent's span with them,
so none reads as stalled.

A notice of a loop counts the traces of its agent that called its tool as often:
"3 traces of support-agent called search 12 or more times in a row, this one 15".

## Starter alerts

A project's first trace makes three alerts for it, so it is watched before
anyone writes one:

| Starter | What it reads | Alerts | Warns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failing traces | The share of the last hour's traces that ended in error | Above 10% | Above 5% |
| Looping traces | The most times in a row a trace of the last hour called one tool ([loops and stalls](#loops-and-stalls)) | At 10 or more | — |
| Stalled traces | The traces left open longer than the last hour | At 1 or more | — |

Each reads every agent of the project, reads a window with no trace as 0, tells
the project's feed alone until you link a connector to it, and is evaluated
every five minutes rather than every minute. Each says **Starter** beside its
severity and is edited, paused, muted and deleted as any alert is; one deleted
is never made again. A trace's cost and duration differ too much from one agent
to the next for one number to start from, so no starter reads them: start
them from an agent's history with **New alert**, or keep an
[objective](/docs/monitoring/objectives).

Once the project has run for 14 days, **Failing traces** takes its own history,
as **From its history** offers it: its alert threshold the 99th percentile of
the share of an hour's traces that failed over the last 14 whole UTC days, in
whole percents up and at least 1%, and its warning the 90th, where that is
lower. A project with no trace in those days keeps 10% and 5%, and one you
edited first keeps yours. It is set once.

The project's feed is told what its first trace made, and the making and the
threshold its history sets are recorded in the
[audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log) as `service:starter-alerts`. On a
deployment that sells plans, a starter counts in the plan's alerts as any
alert does: one that would pass the plan's number is not made, and the
**Alerts** page names it with the plan's own words on what holds more.

## Maintenance windows

**Mute** on an alert, or **Mute every alert** at the top of the page once the
project has one, opens a window over it: from now or a time you set, for 30
minutes up to 30 days. **Mute for an hour** on an incident's notice in Slack
opens one over its alert, or its [monitor](/docs/monitoring/monitors), from
now, for an hour. A window over every alert holds the project's monitors too.
While it holds, the alert is evaluated as ever and its incidents open and
resolve in the feed, but no connector hears them, save that a connector which
heard an incident before the window hears it resolve. An incident still open,
and not acknowledged, when the window ends is told once to each connector it
kept the incident from. **End** ends one
early, and one still to come before it starts. A window over every alert
stands at the head of the alerts, and with each alert. A project holds at most
50 windows that have not ended; end one to set another. It keeps the newest
200 that have ended, the oldest going as another opens or ends; the
[audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log) keeps who opened each, and who ended
one early.

## From a trace

A notice that opens, reopens or raises an incident names the trace that best
shows it — the latest that failed, the slowest call, the one with the most
tokens, the one that spent the most, the one with the most steps, the one that
called one tool the most times in a row, the one quiet longest, or the one
scored lowest for an alert below its threshold and highest above it, as the
alert measures, and the latest for a count — and links its trace. That trace's page links back, beside its
tags, to each incident whose notices showed it, under its alert on the Alerts
page, even an incident too old for the page's 200. An incident keeps the last
twenty traces its notices showed, so a trace shown before those links back no
more.
