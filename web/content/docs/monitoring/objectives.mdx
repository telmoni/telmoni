An **objective** promises that a share of one agent's runs succeed — 99% of
`support-bot`'s runs over the last 30 days — or end inside a time: 95% of
them inside 2,000 ms. The runs that miss it — for success, those that ended
in error; for a time, those that ran past it — spend its **error budget**, the share it lets miss: 1% of the
runs for a 99% objective. A project's **Objectives** page
(`/{organization}/{project}/objectives`) lists them, and any member of the
project makes, edits, pauses, mutes and deletes them, as they do
[alerts](/docs/monitoring/alerts).

## Burn rate

How fast an objective's budget goes is its **burn**: the share of its runs
that missed it over a window, over the share it allows. A burn of 1 spends
the budget in exactly its days; at 2 it is gone in half of them.

Every minute each objective's burn is read twice, from its agent's runs:

| Window | Past | Then |
|---|---|---|
| The last hour | 14.4× — 2% of a 30 days' budget gone in an hour | An incident at **Alert**, its page |
| The last six hours | 6× — 5% of a 30 days' budget gone in six hours | An incident at **Warning** |

The incident is told in the project's feed and to the connectors the
objective links, as an alert's is, and resolves once both burns are under
their thresholds again. A run counts in the window it landed in; a window with
no run spends nothing. Each notice says what the objective promises, the burn
that crossed, and how soon the budget is gone at that rate.

## The list

| Column | What it says |
|---|---|
| Name | The objective, where it stands, and a link to its incidents on the Alerts page |
| Promise | What it promises, in words |
| Budget left | The share of its budget its runs' misses have left over its days, below 0 once spent past it, with how many of how many runs missed. On a plan that keeps the spans fewer days, over as many as it keeps |
| Last hour | Its burn over the last hour |
| Last six hours | Its burn over the last six hours, and, past 1, how soon the budget is gone at the faster of the two |

## Making an objective

**New objective** opens the form over the page.

| Field | What it takes |
|---|---|
| Name | 1 to 60 characters; a project holds one alert or objective of a name, whatever its case |
| Agent | The one agent whose runs it promises, as its spans name it |
| Its runs | **Succeed**, or **End inside a time** |
| Inside | For a latency, the time in milliseconds, 1 to 3,600,000. Its 50th, 90th and 99th percentile run durations over the last 14 whole days are offered as buttons, each saying what it replaces |
| Promised | The share of its runs, 50% to 99.99% |
| Over | The last 7, 28 or 30 days |
| Notify again | Every 1 to 10,080 minutes while an incident holds at one severity and nobody has acknowledged it; empty for never |
| Connectors | The project's Slack channels and webhooks it tells, as an [alert's](/docs/monitoring/alerts#making-an-alert) do |

An objective is the alert on its burn it is: on a deployment that sells
plans it counts as one of the organization's alerts, it is listed on the
Alerts page too, and its incidents are acknowledged and resolved there, under
it, as any alert's are.
