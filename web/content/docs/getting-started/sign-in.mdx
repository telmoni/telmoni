How you sign in depends on the deployment. A self-hosted instance offers a
password form, an OpenID Connect identity provider, or both. The hosted service
at telmoni.com has the password form switched off and signs you in through its
identity provider (listed on the [Subprocessors](/legal/subprocessors) page),
so the native-account sections below do not apply there. The CLI signs in
through device authorization on either.

## Native accounts

On standalone and self-hosted instances using Telmoni's built-in authentication:

- **Sign up**: Offered where the operator allows it (`ALLOW_SIGN_UP=true`; it
  is off by default, and then only an address holding an invitation can
  register). Enter your email address, a password (8 characters at least,
  256 bytes at most), and, optionally, your first and last name. When the
  deployment sets `VERIFY_EMAIL=true`, Telmoni emails a confirmation link that
  expires in 24 hours, and the account cannot sign in until you have opened it
  and pressed the button on the **Confirm your email address** page. Signing in
  before that is refused and sends a fresh link; at most five verification
  links go to an account a day.
- **Sign in**: Authenticate with your email and password. Five consecutive
  incorrect attempts lock the account for 5 minutes.
- **Password reset**: Selecting **Forgot your password?** emails a single-use
  reset link valid for 60 minutes when the address holds a password account,
  and the page answers the same either way; at most five links go out a day.
  Using the link terminates all active sessions and records the address as
  verified, since the link came from it.

Passwords are salted and hashed using Argon2id.

## Identity providers (OIDC)

A deployment can configure an external OpenID Connect identity provider
(`OIDC_ISSUER`): one that publishes its discovery document at
`{issuer}/.well-known/openid-configuration` and signs ID tokens with RS256.
Telmoni sends you to the provider to sign in, verifies the ID token it returns
(its signature, issuer, audience and expiry), and creates or links the local
account; a password, if the provider asks for one, is entered at the provider.

Beside the password form, the sign-in page shows **Continue with** and the
provider's name (`OIDC_NAME`, "single sign-on" unless set); with
`DISABLE_LOGIN_FORM=true`, signing in goes straight to the provider. Telmoni
asks the provider for the `openid`, `profile` and `email` scopes, and takes your name, and whether your address is
verified, from what it answers; a sign-in the provider shares no address for is
refused. A first sign-in creates your account while
`OIDC_ALLOW_SIGN_UP` is on; an address that already holds an account here is
refused unless the operator has set
`OIDC_ALLOW_INSECURE_EMAIL_LOOKUP` (see
[Production](/docs/self-host/production#identity--authentication-production-oidc-hardening)).

## Verified addresses

Your account records whether your address has been proved, and the console
reads that record rather than the session. An address counts as verified once
you confirm it on the page a password sign-up's confirmation link opens, use a
password reset link, confirm an email change with the code the new address
received (on the hosted service, it is recorded as its identity provider
reports it), or sign in through an identity provider that vouches for it; the
seeded `ADMIN_EMAIL` account starts verified. A password sign-up on a
deployment with `VERIFY_EMAIL` off is not verified, and a provider's sign-in
records what the provider claims, keeping a proof already recorded for the same
address.

What the record gates is the inbox's word: the console lists invitations to
your address, and accepts them with a press, only while it is verified, and its
live stream carries no invitation to an unverified one. The link in an
invitation email needs only that you sign in, or register, with the exact
address it was sent to; the link is itself the proof. With `VERIFY_EMAIL` on,
an unverified password account cannot use the console at all until its
address is confirmed on the page its link opens, and a sign-in through an
identity provider is refused while the address it brings has not been proved,
by this sign-in or an earlier one.

## Device authentication (CLI)

To sign in from terminal sessions, SSH hosts, or the [Telmoni CLI](/docs/api/cli), Telmoni implements
the OAuth 2.0 Device Authorization Grant ([RFC 8628](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8628)):

1. The CLI initiates the authorization flow via `telmoni login`, displays a one-time code
   (eight consonants, shown as `XXXX-XXXX`, good for ten minutes) and the address of the
   console's device page (`/auth/device`), and opens that page in your browser with the
   code filled in (`/auth/device?code=...`).
2. Open the page in any browser, on any machine. If you are not signed in there, the console
   asks you to sign in first and returns you to the page.
3. Confirm that the code on the page matches what your terminal displays, then select
   **Approve**, or **Deny** to refuse it.
4. The CLI, which polls every five seconds, saves its session as soon as you approve. It never
   asks for your password: you approve it in a browser, and it saves the session its poll
   receives. A code nobody approves within ten minutes expires, and `telmoni login` stops with
   an error; you run it again.

See the [Telmoni CLI guide](/docs/api/cli) for full command reference and headless CI login instructions.

## What your first sign-in creates

The first time you sign in, Telmoni provisions **an Organization**, seating
your account as its **Owner** (unless the operator has switched new
organizations off; see below). It is named after the first word of your name,
"Ada's organization" for Ada Lovelace, or "My organization" when your account
has no name; nothing is taken from your email address. The name is what
everyone in the organization sees, and its URL is derived from it once
(`adas-organization`; see [Name and
URL](/docs/workspace/organizations-and-projects#name-and-url)). Nothing is asked
first: you can rename the organization, or change its URL, on its
**Settings** page whenever you like.

Your name is the one your sign-in supplies. On the hosted service, that is the
first and last name on your profile at its identity provider. On a self-hosted
deployment, it is the first and last name you gave the sign-up form, or the
given and family names your OpenID Connect provider sends. The administrator
a deployment seeds from `ADMIN_EMAIL` has no name, so their organization is
"My organization", at `/my-organization` unless another organization already
holds that URL.

Telmoni provisions an organization the same way whenever it finds you in no
active organization, such as after you leave or delete your last one.

It creates no project. The console opens on the organization's **Overview**;
[create the first one](/docs/workspace/organizations-and-projects#creating-a-project)
from the organization's **Projects** page or the switcher in the console
header. API keys and connectors are made inside a project. A later sign-in opens the Overview of
your [default organization](/docs/account/settings#default-organization), or
returns you to the page that sent you to sign in.

Your account is separate from the organization. You can belong to several
organizations at once: ones you own, including any you
[create](/docs/workspace/organizations-and-projects#creating-an-organization), and
ones you were invited to.

## When an operator closes sign-ups

Three switches are involved, and they do different things:

- **New accounts.** `ALLOW_SIGN_UP=false` closes the password form to new
  accounts, and `OIDC_ALLOW_SIGN_UP=false` closes first-time sign-in through
  the identity provider. Existing accounts sign in normally. An invitation
  still brings a new person in through the password form; with
  `OIDC_ALLOW_SIGN_UP=false` the identity provider admits nobody new, invited
  or not.
- **New organizations.** The `signup` feature flag, a row an operator writes
  in the server's database (see [closing new
  organizations](/docs/self-host/production#closing-new-organizations)), stops
  Telmoni provisioning an organization at first sign-in, and takes **New
  organization** out of the console's switcher, so nobody creates one.
  Signed in with no organization (because of that flag, or after leaving or
  deleting your last one while it is off), you see an account-only screen
  offering:
  - **Pending invitations**: Accept or decline invitations to existing
    organizations and projects, when your address is verified; an unverified
    address accepts from the link in the invitation email instead.
  - **Email** and **Password**: Change your address or request a reset link,
    for a password account.
  - **Data collection**: Your analytics preference, on a deployment with an
    analytics provider configured.
  - **Active sessions**: End any of your other sessions.
  - **Delete your account**: Permanently delete your account and personal data.
  - **Sign out**.

## Sessions

Each sign-in generates an active session backed by an HTTP-only cookie. Its
access token lasts 15 minutes and is renewed in the background while a console
tab is open, or on your next request, against a refresh token that lasts 30
days and is replaced at every renewal; a session unused for 30 days ends. Your
account's **Privacy** page (`/account/privacy`) lists every session not yet
signed out or revoked, one that lapsed after 30 days unused included, with
device details and when each was last active. You
can revoke any other session remotely, and the device signs out within a few
minutes.

Changing your email address ends all your sessions immediately, and so does
resetting your password on a self-hosted deployment. A reset at the hosted
service's identity provider does not.

## Signing out

**Sign out** in the account menu is a form post to `/auth/logout`. It revokes
the session in the database, so an access token still in flight stops working,
clears the console's cookies, and sends the browser home, or, for a session
that began at an identity provider that publishes an `end_session_endpoint`,
through the provider's sign-out page and back to the home page. On the hosted
service, it goes through its identity provider's sign-out page, which ends
your session there too. A post from
another site, which the browser's Fetch Metadata or `Origin` header gives
away, is turned back to the home page without signing you out, and so is a
link to `/auth/logout` from another site.
