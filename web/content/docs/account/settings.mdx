Your account's **Settings** page (`/account/settings`) manages your personal
account: your credentials, and the organization a sign-in opens. These
settings belong to your identity alone and apply across all organizations you
belong to.

## Profile

The **Profile** card shows your **Name**, **Email**, **Sign-in method** (when
your sign-in reported one, which a self-hosted OpenID Connect sign-in does
not), and **User ID**, with a copy button on the ID. With no name on your
account, **Name** shows the part of your address before the `@`. The name is
the one you gave at sign-up, or the one your identity provider sends with each
sign-in; the console has no field to change it. It appears on your member row
and on the invitations you send; the audit log records your user ID. Your
first sign-in named your organization after its first word ("Ada's
organization"), or "My organization" when you had none; a different name at a
later sign-in does not rename it.

## Changing your email address

The **Email** and **Password** sections appear for an account that signs in
with an email address and a password: a password account on a self-hosted
deployment or, on the hosted service, an account that signs in to its identity
provider with a password. An account that signs in any other way, such as
single sign-on, changes its address with its identity provider, and the
sections do not appear.

Updating your email requires two 6-digit verification codes:

1. Enter the new email address; the one already on your account is refused.
2. Two 6-digit codes are sent:
   - One to your **current** email address.
   - One to your **new** email address.
3. Enter both verification codes into the console. Both codes expire after
   **15 minutes**, and five wrong guesses burn them; **Resend codes** issues a
   new pair, and **Use a different address** starts over. Codes can be
   requested five times a day and three times an hour, **Resend codes**
   included. On the hosted service, the new address's code comes from its
   identity provider and follows that provider's expiry and attempt limits.

On a self-hosted deployment, an address another account already holds is
refused with a `409` when you ask for the codes, and again at confirmation if
another account took it in the meantime. On the hosted service, its identity
provider decides whether the address is free.

When the email update succeeds:
- **All active sessions are terminated immediately** (including the current
  session). You must sign in again with your new email address.
- On a self-hosted deployment, the new address is recorded as verified: the
  code it received is the proof. On the hosted service, it is recorded as its
  identity provider reports it.
- Any pending deletion codes or sensitive confirmation codes issued to the old
  email address are invalidated.

## Resetting your password

Select **Email me a password reset link**. On a self-hosted deployment, a
single-use link valid for **60 minutes** is emailed to you, at most five a
day. Using it ends all existing sessions, updates the stored Argon2id password
hash, and records your address as verified, since the link came from it.

On the hosted service, the link comes from its identity provider, which holds
the password, and follows that provider's rules; using it does not end your
Telmoni sessions.

## Default organization

The **Default organization** section names the organization a sign-in opens:
signing in lands on its **Overview** (`/{organization}`), unless a page sent you
to sign in, which it returns you to. A `telmoni login` through the browser
saves the same organization as the [CLI's](/docs/api/cli) active one. To change
it, choose another of the organizations you belong to and select **Save**; the
choice is recorded in that organization's [audit log](/docs/workspace/audit-log).

Until you choose one, the default is the first organization you joined among
those you own or, if you own none, the first you joined. A choice belongs to
your membership, not to the organization:

- Leaving the organization, or being removed from it, clears the choice, and
  the default falls back as above. Joining it again does not restore it.
- While the organization waits out its deletion, it is passed over, and it is
  your default again if it is restored.

## Interface preferences

The user menu in the console header sets display preferences, which this
browser keeps:

- **Theme**: Select **System theme** (synchronizes with your operating system's
  appearance), **Light theme**, or **Dark theme**.
- **Corners**: Choose **Rounded corners** or **Sharp corners** for the console.
- **Keyboard shortcuts**: Select **Keyboard shortcuts** from the user menu, or
  press `?` while single-key shortcuts are on, to see the shortcuts and switch
  single-key navigation on or off.
